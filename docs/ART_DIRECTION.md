# Eagle Defense — Art Direction v1.0

Status: **APPROVED as working direction** (owner, 2026-11-26). Final art direction is confirmed after the G3 visual slice.

## 1. Target look

A polished 16/32-bit-era top-down arcade look. Think a premium late-SNES / GBA / arcade-board era game, not NES. It should feel crisp, saturated and readable, with restrained modern lighting. It should not look realistic, flat-vector or mobile-style.

## 2. Readability rules (non-negotiable)

1. Gameplay sprites have strong silhouettes and a 1 px dark outline facing the background.
2. **Faction colors are reserved:**
   - P1 is warm gold/yellow and P2 is green-teal.
   - Enemies are cool grey-steel.
   - Power-up carriers flash with a red/white pulse.
   - Armor hit stages use a dedicated ramp.
   - Terrain never uses these hues at high saturation.
3. Bullets and power-ups have the highest value contrast on screen.
4. VFX never cover the HUD, and they never hide a bullet longer than 2 frames. The particle count per explosion is capped.
5. Forest is dense but has a subtle hint at its edges so that hidden tanks stay *fair*, similar to the classic.
6. Every effect can be disabled: screen shake, bloom, CRT and flashes. There is a reduced-flash accessibility option.

## 3. Technical art spec (hypothesis, validated in G3)

| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Internal resolution | 640×360, integer-scaled |
| Tile | 24×24 px, with destruction cells of 6×6 px |
| Tanks | 24×24 px; 4 directions (generated by rotation, then hand-corrected); 2–3 track animation frames |
| Bullets | 4×6 px with a 2–3 px trail |
| HQ | 24×24 px (1 tile), with an intact state and a destroyed state |
| Power-ups | 20×20 px icons on a pulsing plate |
| Palette | Master palette of ≤ 48 colors (`assets/palette.gpl`); every environment uses a subset plus a tint |
| Fonts | Original bitmap font, or an OFL-licensed pixel font (tracked in provenance) |
| Rendering | Nearest filtering, no sub-pixel sprite positions, no rotation or scale on gameplay sprites |

## 4. Environments

| # | Environment | Ground | Mood | Accent lighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Classic Brick | dark asphalt | neutral arcade | warm muzzle flashes |
| 2 | Countryside | grass/dirt | daylight | soft sun tint |
| 3 | Industrial | concrete, hazard stripes | overcast | sodium lamps |
| 4 | Winter | snow, ice | cold | blue ambient, white bloom |
| 5 | Stronghold | scorched earth, rubble | dusk | fire glow |

Brick, steel, water, forest and ice have **the same shape language** in every environment, so a player who has learned one environment can read all five. Only the texture and tint change.

## 5. IP distance (original identity)

We share game *mechanics* with the reference games, but never their *look*:

- No reproduction of the reference tank sprites, eagle emblem, brick/steel tile patterns, HUD layout (the grey right-hand panel with enemy icons), title logo or palette.
- The HQ is an **original stylized eagle emblem**, for example a geometric eagle on a hexagonal plinth, designed from scratch.
- The HUD uses our own side-panel layout and iconography.
- References are used only for *gameplay readability*. We never use reference sprites as tracing or palette sources.

## 6. Effects list (presentation only)

Muzzle flash, projectile trail, brick debris chunks, steel sparks, tank explosion (flash, fireball, smoke, scorch decal), HQ destruction sequence, track marks that fade over about 3 s, spawn marker, shield shimmer, freeze tint, the shovel steel transition, light sprites (muzzle, explosions, HQ beacon), capped bloom, optional screen shake (≤ 2 px, off when reduced motion is on), and optional CRT.

## 7. Asset production and validation approach

**Pipeline A (default): source-defined pixel art in the repo.**

- Sprites are authored as text-indexed pixel maps plus a palette (`assets/src/*.lua`). A tool (`tools/build_sprites.lua`) renders them to PNG atlases. The source is diffable and reviewable, and has clear provenance.
- This is how the AI agent produces original art. The quality ceiling is good for tiles, effects and UI. Hero sprites (tanks, HQ) are the risk.

**Pipeline B (owner option): a human pixel artist**, commissioned for hero sprites if the G3 slice shows Pipeline A falls short. The artist delivers `.aseprite` sources plus a written commercial license assignment.

**Audio:**

- SFX come from a seeded synthesizer tool (`tools/sfx/`). The parameters are stored in the repo, and the tool renders to WAV.
- Music is composed in a tracker-style data format and rendered offline to OGG, or commissioned.

**Validation gates (automated where possible):**

1. `assets/PROVENANCE.md` and a machine-readable `assets/manifest.lua` record, for every shipped file: author, tool, license and source. CI fails on any unlisted asset.
2. `tools/check_assets.lua` validates the files: PNG dimensions are multiples of the grid, colors are within the palette, there is no stray alpha, and atlas sizes are within limits.
3. Each milestone with art includes screenshots at 1× and 3×, viewed by the owner on a real display.
4. **Steam AI-content disclosure.** Assets produced by an AI agent (including code-generated pixel art) must be disclosed in the Steam content survey. This is tracked as a G9 task.
5. Game-made assets are proprietary and must not be released under the code license (see `NOTICE.md`).
6. External packs or fonts are allowed only with licenses that permit commercial use (CC0, OFL, or an explicit commercial license), and must be recorded in provenance.
